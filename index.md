## How an engagement works

Each engagement runs like a branch in a codebase.

1. **Take the open chair.** A company has urgent work and nobody assigned to it, usually the sales motion or a product that needs to reach launch. Alex takes ownership of it.
2. **Build it and run it.** Alex makes the pieces directly, from the CRM to the launch plan, and keeps running them until they work without outside help.
3. **Hand back the seat.** When the founder or a full-time hire is ready, they inherit a working function and a playbook written for them.

## Recent work

### Dispatch Care: founder enablement (go-to-market)

Dispatch Care is an early-stage healthcare startup where the founder was doing all the selling. Alex's role: GTM / BizOps Lead.

- **ICP and buyer personas.** Segmented target accounts by geography, bed count, and service mix, and mapped the buying decision makers: CNOs, CNIOs, and COOs. The founder came away with a defined account list and a clear picture of who to talk to at each one.
- **Positioning and sales collateral.** Wrote a pitch deck aimed at CNOs, a one-pager to leave behind after meetings, and a case study with clinical outcome claims that could be defended. Each piece was built around what a CNO needs to win support from colleagues inside the hospital.
- **Campaign strategy.** Laid out which campaigns to run against the account list and in what order: warm introductions first, cold outreach only after those were used up. Ran the outreach directly at the start, from account research through to booked meetings.
- **Conference prep.** Prepared the founder for industry conferences, using the ICP and positioning work to decide who was worth meeting and what to say.
- **Pipeline in a CRM.** Set up the CRM from scratch with defined pipeline stages and a repeatable path from first contact to booked meeting. Before that, the pipeline lived in the CEO's head.

Result: the founder runs a defined sales motion.

### Kroot: taking a product to launch

Kroot is a medical education app that was stuck before launch. Alex's role: Product Lead.

- **Product audit.** Walked the full user flow, from onboarding through spaced repetition to payment, and logged every critical bug, UX problem, and gap between build and spec. Delivered a prioritized backlog with severity ratings and acceptance criteria.
- **Launch plan.** Wrote a 90-day launch plan in phases, each with exit criteria. Work was split by workstream (product, content, development, user research), each with an owner.
- **Payment strategy.** Compared App Store first, web first, and hybrid. The decision memo covered build complexity, expected conversion, regional compliance, and time to revenue. The team chose web first.
- **User research.** Ran 10 structured discovery sessions with medical students in the target market, covering subject priorities, willingness to pay, and actual study behavior.
- **Closed beta.** Ran a 20-user closed beta with structured feedback, weekly synthesis, and severity-based bug triage. Set launch metrics in advance: activation, 7-day and 30-day retention, and session frequency.

Result: live users in a new market.

### National clinical contact center network: reorganization (business operations)

A national clinical contact center network with 170+ sites was going through a major reorganization. Alex built the workforce and staffing models leadership used to plan it.

- **Workforce mapping.** Mapped the client's workforce data to its core service lines, then widened the scope to leadership and administrative support roles. Executive roles were left out and data anomalies were flagged for review.
- **One staffing method everywhere.** Applied a single full-time-equivalent staffing method across departments using regional network data, with clear inclusion rules so every role was counted the same way.
- **Future-state staffing model.** Took a current state of 15 to 20 tracked positions per service down to a future-state shell of 4 to 5 positions per service.
- **Regional consolidation.** Leadership was consolidating 18 regional networks into 5. Alex allocated headcount to the new regions using each facility's share of regional enrollment as a stand-in for demand.
- **Supervisor ratio scenarios.** Built an adjustable Excel model testing supervisor-to-staff ratios of 1:10, 1:12, and 1:16, documented known data gaps, and delivered the executive-ready model to the client's project sponsor.

Result: leadership got 3 to 5 costed options for the reorganization.
